Retrieval-Augmented Generation, or RAG, improves a chatbot by adding an external knowledge step before generation.
The system first loads documents, splits them into chunks, and stores those chunks in a searchable index.
When a user asks a question, the retriever finds chunks that are similar to the question.
The chatbot then sends the user's question plus the retrieved chunks to the language model.
This makes the answer more grounded in the uploaded material and reduces hallucination.

In a classroom demo, RAG is useful because students can see the difference between model memory and document-grounded answering.
The retrieval step is separate from the generation step.
Chunk size and overlap affect retrieval quality.
Small chunks are precise but may miss context.
Large chunks contain more context but may add irrelevant text.
