Retrieval-Augmented Generation, or RAG, is a pattern for grounding language model answers in external documents.
The pipeline has two major phases: indexing and querying.
During indexing, documents are loaded, split into chunks, embedded as vectors, and stored in a vector database.
During querying, the user question is embedded, similar chunks are retrieved, and the language model receives both the question and the retrieved context.

Chunking strategy affects answer quality.
Small chunks can be precise but may lose surrounding context.
Large chunks preserve context but may include irrelevant information.
Sentence-window chunking is useful when documents contain short factual statements.
Paragraph packing is useful when paragraphs are well structured.
Recursive separators try to preserve natural boundaries before falling back to smaller chunks.

Embedding models convert text into numeric vectors.
Keyword vectors such as TF-IDF are easy to explain and fast for demos.
Neural embeddings from OpenAI, Ollama, or Hugging Face capture semantic similarity beyond exact word overlap.
Vector databases such as Qdrant store vectors and perform nearest-neighbor search efficiently.
